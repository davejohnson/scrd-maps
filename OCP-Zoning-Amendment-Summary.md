# Roberts Creek OCP & Zoning Amendment Precedents — Summary for Subdivision Planning

## Your Proposal at a Glance

You want to take a 1-acre (~4000 m2) property in Roberts Creek and:

- **Split off ~1/2 acre** (~2000 m2) containing the existing house and septic, and rezone that portion from Residential C / Subdivision District E to **Residential A / Subdivision District C**
- **Merge the other ~1/2 acre** with a neighbouring property

This requires two bylaw amendments:
1. **OCP Amendment (Bylaw 641.xx)** — change the land use designation on the subdivided portion from Residential C (min 5000 m2) to Residential A (min 2000 m2)
2. **Zoning Amendment (Bylaw 722.xx)** — change the subdivision district from E (min 4000 m2, avg 5000 m2) to C (min 2000 m2)

The R2 zoning itself does not need to change.

## Precedent Case 1: Topping — 2720 Lower Rd (OCP 641.10 / Zoning 310.179)

**Nearly identical to your situation.** This is your strongest precedent.

### The Application
- **Property:** 5800 m2 triangular parcel at Woodley Rd / Lower Rd intersection
- **Existing:** Residential C / R2 zone / Subdivision District E
- **Proposed:** Residential A / R2 zone / Subdivision District C
- **Goal:** Subdivide into two parcels (~2800 m2 and ~2900 m2, or ~2400 m2 and ~3400 m2)
- **Applicant:** Brian & Cheryl Topping (residents since 2000)
- **Planner:** David Rafael (1st reading), Yuli Siao (2nd/3rd)

### Timeline (18 months)
| Date | Stage |
|------|-------|
| Apr 12, 2018 | PCD Committee — Staff report recommends First Reading |
| Apr 26, 2018 | Board — First Reading given |
| Mar 12, 2019 | Public Information Meeting (hosted by applicant, 5 attendees) |
| May 9, 2019 | PCD Committee — Staff recommends Second Reading |
| May 23, 2019 | Board — Second Reading given |
| Jul 9, 2019 | Public Hearing at Roberts Creek Community Hall |
| Sep 12, 2019 | PCD Committee — Staff recommends Third Reading & Adoption |

### What Worked
- **Septic evidence was the key unlock.** VCH confirmed each proposed lot could meet subdivision guidelines. The applicant hired a Registered Onsite Wastewater Practitioner (Brian Newman, ROWP) who provided soil measurements, percolation tests, and proposed covenant areas. Wet-season monitoring was done.
- **APC was supportive.** The Roberts Creek APC noted: little exposed rock on the property, significant soil depth, seven neighbouring lots already under 2000 m2, and modern septic technology can handle smaller lots.
- **OCP Committee supported it**, including allowing full development potential (not restricting to one dwelling per new lot).
- **Neighbour support.** Public info meeting attendees were generally supportive; smaller lots = more affordable housing.
- **Staff framed it as consistent with OCP densification policies** (Section 18): subdivisions creating 3 lots or fewer in Residential designations outside village hubs are supported if they meet criteria for access, transit, fire protection, water service, sewage disposal, and compatibility.
- **Ministry of Transportation** granted preliminary approval (required because property is within 800m of a controlled-access highway).
- **Squamish Nation** had no issues.

### Hurdles
- **The 18-month timeline.** First Reading to Third Reading took a year and a half. Budget time for this.
- **Opposition at the public hearing.** Three residents spoke/wrote against it, concerned about density increase outside the village core, traffic, and servicing impacts. Staff addressed these by citing OCP Section 18 densification criteria.
- **Septic evidence required before Second Reading.** Staff explicitly required the applicant to demonstrate septic capability before they would schedule a public hearing. This is non-negotiable.
- **MOTI approval required before adoption.** Because the property is within 800m of Highway 101, the Ministry of Transportation must approve under Section 52 of the Transportation Act. This was a condition of Third Reading.

### Staff's Key Arguments (useful for your application)
- OCP Section 18 densification policies provide criteria to evaluate density increases in residential areas outside village cores
- Property was 1.6 km from Roberts Creek village hub, 7.5 km from upper Gibsons — accessible via major roads
- Transit stops adjacent to the property
- Within fire protection area and SCRD water service area
- Additional traffic from 2 possible dwellings is "negligible"
- Smaller lots create affordable housing options — many owners of large lots can't afford to build second dwellings, but subdividing creates opportunity for others

### What Worked

## What This Means for Your Application

### Your situation compared to the Topping precedent

| Factor | Topping (641.10) | Your Proposal |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| Current OCP | Residential C | Residential C |
| Proposed OCP | Residential A | Residential A |
| Current Subdivision District | E | E |
| Proposed Subdivision District | C | C |
| Current Zoning | R2 | R2 |
| Proposed Zoning | R2 (no change) | R2 (no change) |
| Parcel size | 5800 m2 | ~4000 m2 (1 acre) |
| Resulting lots | 2 new lots (~2400-3400 m2 each) | ~2000 m2 (kept) + remainder merged with neighbour |

**Your proposal is actually simpler than Topping's** in one important way: you're not creating a net new lot. You're splitting your property in two and merging half with the neighbour. The existing house and septic stay on the retained portion. This may reduce concerns about density increase and new servicing demands.

### Critical Path — What You Need to Do

1. **Septic assessment first.** Hire a ROWP to confirm the ~2000 m2 lot with the existing house and septic meets VCH subdivision guidelines. This is the gate that must be cleared before Second Reading. Since you already have a functioning septic system, this should be straightforward — but you need the formal assessment.

2. **Confirm the 2000 m2 lot meets Bylaw 722 Section 4.3.3.** For parcels under 3500 m2 but at least 2000 m2, you need a minimum of 1000 m2 of continuous developable area (excluding panhandles, rights-of-way, and streamside protection areas).

3. **Check if you're within 800m of Highway 101.** If so, you'll need MOTI approval under Section 52 of the Transportation Act before adoption. The Topping property needed this.

4. **Engage the Roberts Creek APC early.** Both precedent cases show the APC was supportive. Their recommendation carries weight with the PCD Committee.

5. **Check Section 4.5.1(c) — lot line relocation exception.** Since you're merging half your lot with the neighbour, part of this may qualify as a "lot line relocation to facilitate existing development or improve parcel layout" which exempts minimum parcel area requirements *if no additional parcels are created*. Discuss this with SCRD planning staff — it could simplify the process for the merger portion.

6. **Note Section 5.23.1(a) — Electoral Area D split-zoned parcel rule.** In Area D specifically, the zone permitting the *least* number of dwellings (using total parcel area) determines the max dwellings. This is more restrictive than other electoral areas.

### Estimated Timeline
Based on precedents: **12-18 months** from application to adoption. Budget for:
- 2-3 months for referrals after First Reading
- Public Information Meeting (you host, notify neighbours within 100m)
- 1-2 months between Second Reading and Public Hearing
- 1-3 months between Public Hearing and Third Reading

### Referral Agencies
Your application will be referred to:
- Roberts Creek Advisory Planning Commission (APC)
- Squamish Nation (Skwxwu7mesh)
- Ministry of Transportation & Infrastructure (if within 800m of Hwy 101)
- Vancouver Coastal Health Authority
